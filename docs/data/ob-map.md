## Unit classes, counter strength and loss structure

Source: OCR §§3.1–3.5, 4.21–4.22, 4.45–4.46, 9.26, 11.3, 12.18–12.19, 14.4, 15.83–15.84.

| Framework item | Published structure / numerical rule |
|---|---|
| Counter information | Land combat counters identify unit, organizational size, type, parent and stacking value; combat ratings and current TOE are kept on OA/TOE sheets, not printed as counter attack/defense strengths |
| Strength granularity | Counter contains a variable number of integral TOE strength points; strength reductions remove TOE points, not an assumed universal two-step counter sequence |
| TOE scale, glossary §3.1 | Approximately 100 men, 5–8 armored vehicles, or 4–8 guns per TOE point |
| TOE scale, characteristics §3.5 | Infantry approximately 100–200 men depending on function; tanks 5–8 vehicles; gun battery 4 guns per TOE point. These are the two published approximations, not a single exact headcount conversion |
| Arrival indicators | `N`: normal maximum; `U@x`: x TOE points understrength; `O@x`: x TOE points overstrength. Weapon units list counts by weapon type |
| Raw strength | Sum `TOE points committed × rating for the combat function` over the participating weapon components |
| Actual strength, barrage / anti-armor / close assault | `round-half-up(total raw / 10)`; aggregate raw before dividing, including eligible attacks from different hexes against the same target |
| Rounding examples | 11.4 → 11; 11.5 → 12; raw ≤4 normally → actual 0 |
| Small close assault / probe | If each side has raw <10, use raw as actual (§11.33; also §§15.28, 15.51) |
| Actual AA | `TOE × AA rating`, without dividing by 10; rating 4 × TOE 2 = 8 actual AA points |
| Armor protection | Constant per-TOE damage-absorption value, not scaled/divided as combat strength; remove enough armored TOE points for the sum of their protection values to cover the damage |
| Armor example (§14.43) | 7 damage = remove 1 M/13 TOE at protection 3 and 2 M/11 TOE at protection 2 each; 3 + 2 + 2 = 7 |
| Excess anti-armor damage | Damage remaining after all eligible armor is destroyed is ignored |
| Self-propelled gun anti-armor losses | Absorption per TOE = armor protection + vulnerability, using the applicable modified vulnerability |
| Halftracks alone as armor targets | Double their armor protection when they are the only eligible anti-armor targets; loss removes infantry TOE and its transporting truck point(s) |
| Gun vulnerability | Constant per-TOE loss-absorption rating; higher is less vulnerable. Forward guns lose enough vulnerability points to cover at least 50% of raw close-assault points lost; any raw loss requires at least 1 vulnerability point lost; AA/Flak exempt |
| Gun overrun | All defending guns, Forward or Back, are subject to vulnerability losses after percentage losses |
| Artillery in offensive close assault | Vulnerability is halved and rounded upward: 7 → 4 |
| Artillery firing anti-armor | Vulnerability becomes 2 |
| Close-assault percentage losses | Apply percentage to raw assault strength; attacker rounds lost raw points upward, defender downward; remove TOE whose applicable off/def assault values absorb those raw losses (§15.83, subject to §15.77) |
| Shell battalion | Less than 50% of maximum TOE; artillery shell threshold is less than 25% (§9.26) |
| Basic morale scale | +3 through −3 (§3.5); morale is separate from weapon ratings |

### Terrain chart qualifications

| Note | Independently phrased numerical / operational qualification |
|---|---|
| 1 | Garrison occupies 0 stacking in its assigned hex. AA units occupy 0 stacking in a major city with no count limit; up to 3 AA units in an airfield and 1 in an air landing strip may also occupy 0 stacking |
| 2 | Vehicles other than light trucks, recce-type and motorcycle infantry require a track to enter/leave salt marsh. No motorized combat into/out of salt marsh. §8.44 also explicitly permits road movement for restricted vehicles |
| 3 | Light trucks, motorcycle recce and motorcycle infantry cannot enter desert even along a track |
| 4 | Alexandria and Cairo are fortification level 3; other major cities are level 2. The scan's footnote 4 is printed next to “Swamp,” although the note itself names cities; retained without silently relocating the marker |
| 5 | Commonwealth railroad movement may use §8.7 rather than ordinary hex movement |
| 6 | Road cancels hexside crossing CP and breakdown costs |
| 7 | Road/track stacking restriction concerns the units/truck points actually on that route in the hex at that time |
| 8 | Track halves terrain-feature entry costs and hex/hexside breakdown values; vehicle descent of an escarpment is excepted, so its +8 CP and +6 breakdown are not halved |
| 9 | Motorized/trucks can descend an escarpment only on a track |
| 10 | During rainstorm, wadi crossing requires road/railroad; crossing adds 2 CP. Railroad counts as road for this exception |
| 11 | The motorized major-river prohibition is lifted at road/railroad crossings, with no added CP or breakdown cost |
| 12 | Armor gets the marked barrage defensive fortification benefit only in a major-city hex |
| 13 | Engineers can reduce minefield entry costs (§26.2). If attackers occupy an enemy minefield, the non-phasing force receives L1 anti-armor and close-assault shifts unless already gaining those shifts from its friendly minefield |
